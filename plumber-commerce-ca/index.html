An Industrial City With Real Plumbing Needs

Commerce was incorporated in 1960 and is described as an industrial city, home to warehouses, shops and a few residential neighborhoods. One landmark is The Citadel, a former tire factory that is now an outlet center. The city's water comes from its own system, which Cal Water operates as part of its East Los Angeles District.

For plumbing, a city like this raises two questions. Does the building have floor drains, a grease interceptor or a backflow device that needs testing? And who approves repairs: an owner, a tenant or a manager? The answers change which plumber we send, so tell dispatch when you call. Commercial plumbing explains the scope.

Atlantic Boulevard connects Commerce to Maywood, so dispatch from Slauson Avenue reaches most of the city without crossing the freeway network.

What The Cal Water Report Shows

Cal Water's 2025 consumer confidence report for the City of Commerce system says the water comes from two active local wells and from purchased Metropolitan Water District water. Groundwater total hardness ran from 140 to 155 ppm, with an average of 146 ppm. That is about 8.5 grains per gallon, a figure we calculated from the report.

In practice that is moderately hard water, and it still leaves scale over time. Warehouses with large water heaters and shops with dishwashers feel it first. A scheduled flush can protect equipment, and water heater repair explains what we check.

Before We Arrive: Burst Pipe Or Active Leak

For a leak in a Commerce shop, warehouse or home:

Shut the main

Find the building shutoff and close it. In a multi-tenant building, call the manager about the building main.

Protect stock and equipment

Move pallets, boxes and electronics off the floor and away from the water.

Keep power safe

Do not touch wet electrical gear. Turn off breakers only when you can reach them dry.

Call us at once

Floor Drains, Grease And Backflow

Floor drains

Floor drains in shops and warehouses clog with grit and trash. A plumber clears the line and checks that the trap holds water so odors do not rise.

Grease interceptors

Kitchens that sell food produce grease, and a full interceptor sends it into the building drain. We clean interceptors and can set a schedule.

Backflow devices

Some commercial water supplies must have a backflow preventer tested on a schedule. Ask your supplier what applies, and we can test and repair the device.

Permits And Code In Commerce

Plumbing repairs in Commerce follow the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Water heater replacements, repipes and sewer work usually need a permit from the city building department, and the inspector checks the finished work. Ask your plumber who pulls the permit and keep the sign-off with your records.
