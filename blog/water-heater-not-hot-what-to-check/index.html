Water Heater Not Hot? What To Check Before You Call

Many no-hot-water calls have a simple cause. Here is a safe checklist, and the points where you should stop and call.

Start With Gas Or Electric

Find out which fuel your heater uses. A gas heater has a burner at the bottom and a flue pipe on top. An electric heater has two access panels on the side and no flue. The checks differ, and mixing them up wastes time.

Look at the heater first. If you see water pooling under the tank, shut off the water and the power or gas and call. A leaking tank cannot be repaired. If the tank is dry, work through the list below.

Electric Heater Checklist

  • Check the breaker. Heaters often have a double breaker. Switch it fully off and on once.
  • Press the red reset button on the upper thermostat only after turning power off at the breaker. Do not remove panels with power on.
  • If the breaker trips again right away, stop. A shorted element needs a plumber.
  • If hot water runs out fast, one of two elements may have failed. That is a repair, not a guess.

Hard Groundwater And Heater Life

Cities around Maywood draw groundwater with notable hardness, and city reports for South Gate and Huntington Park list it in the hard range. Scale is a common reason for noise and for short heater life here.

If your heater is old and you hear popping, a flush may help, and the water heater repair page lists what we check.

We take calls from South Gate and Bell as well, so the steps here apply there too.

Gas Heater Checklist

  • Look at the gas control knob. It should be on, not on pilot or off.
  • If the pilot is out, follow the lighting steps printed on the heater. Do not try if you smell gas.
  • A pilot that will not stay lit often points to a worn thermocouple or a draft problem.
  • If you smell gas at any time, leave the house and call the gas company from outside.

Gas heaters also need clear venting. A heater that shuts off and smells of exhaust should be left off until it is checked.

Check The Thermostat And The Relief Valve

A thermostat set too low gives lukewarm water. A common household setting is around 120 degrees, which balances safety and comfort. Do not raise it beyond what the manufacturer allows, because very hot water scalds.

The temperature and pressure relief valve is the safety device on the side of the tank. If it drips constantly, it may be reacting to high pressure or heat. Do not cap it. Call a plumber to test it.

Why Heaters Fail

Most tank heaters last about a decade, though water quality and care change that. Sediment, a spent anode rod and constant high temperature all shorten life. When the tank itself corrodes, it leaks, and no part can fix that.

Heater parts such as elements, thermostats, thermocouples and gas valves wear out sooner than the tank. A repair on a heater with a sound tank is often reasonable. A repair on an old tank that is rusting is usually money spent on borrowed time.

Repair Or Replace: A Plain Rule

If the tank is leaking, replace it. If the tank is sound and the part is cheap, repair it. If the tank is old and you face a repair that costs a large share of a new heater, compare the two prices and the years left. Ask the plumber for both numbers in writing.

Replacement is also a chance to change type. Tankless units heat on demand and take less space. They cost more up front and need descaling in hard water. Water heater installation explains what the work includes, including venting and permits.

Safety Points Worth Repeating

Never bypass the relief valve, never store flammables near a gas heater and never ignore the smell of gas. If the heater and the flue seem hot to the touch, or the burner flame is yellow and lazy instead of blue, switch the heater off and call. These are signs of a venting or burner problem that can produce carbon monoxide.

Carbon monoxide alarms near sleeping areas are a low-cost safeguard in any home with gas appliances.

What To Tell The Plumber

Say whether the heater is gas or electric, how old it is and what changed when the hot water stopped. Mention any noises, smells, leaks or tripped breakers. A plumber who knows the story brings the right parts and finishes faster.

Cold Showers In A Rental

If you rent and the heater fails, tell the landlord at once, in writing if you can. Heating is a basic part of a habitable home, and most landlords need to fix it within a reasonable time. Keep a copy of your message and take a photo of any leak.

Do not try to repair gas appliances in a rental yourself. Leave that to a licensed plumber and let the landlord arrange or approve it.

Recovery Time And Sizing

A heater that works but runs out quickly may be too small for the household. Added bathrooms, a larger family or a jetted tub can outgrow a tank that once fit. Ask the plumber about the first hour rating on the label and compare it to peak use.

Sometimes the answer is a larger tank, and sometimes it is a tankless unit that heats on demand.

The relief valve, its discharge pipe and earthquake bracing are required by the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), so ask the plumber to confirm all three at every heater visit.
