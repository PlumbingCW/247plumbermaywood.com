Commercial Leak Detection: What To Expect

Commercial leak detection involves bigger systems, and an honest estimate takes more than one phone call. A plumber needs to know the number of units, where the meter sits and whether lines are shared. In many commercial buildings a leak search can take longer than in a home, and the equipment used may be different.

We find the leak and explain it, and we are candid when a repair is outside what we do. If the issue is a roof drain or an HVAC line, the plumber tells you, so you can call the right trade rather than pay for a plumbing repair that will not help.

Grease, Backflow And Floor Drains

Grease traps and interceptors

Kitchens produce grease, and a full trap sends it into the building drain. Cleaning schedules depend on the size of the trap and the volume of cooking. Ask your city or your landlord whether a schedule is required for your type of business.

Backflow devices

A backflow preventer protects the public water supply from contamination. Many commercial buildings have one, and some must be tested on a schedule. Ask your water supplier whether your device needs testing, and call us for the work.

Floor drains and storm drains

A floor drain that overflows or smells is usually a clog or a dry trap. Storm drain problems after heavy rain often come from debris and need cleaning before the next storm. Hydro jetting is often the right tool, see hydro jetting.

Safety Steps For A Commercial Leak Or Backup

Before the plumber arrives at a shop, restaurant or warehouse:

  • Close the building main or the tenant shutoff and tell staff and customers to avoid the wet area.
  • Switch off equipment on wet floors only if you can reach the switch while dry.
  • Move stock, boxes and electronics away from the water and off low shelves.
  • Keep food away from any floor drain backup and stop food prep until the line is clear.
  • Call the property manager if the main is shared, then call us with the building type and address.

Code And Rules To Know

Commercial buildings add rules on top of the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Food businesses may need a grease interceptor under local sewer authority rules, and many water supplies require backflow devices tested under California Title 17 cross-connection rules. Ask your supplier and your city which apply to your building.
