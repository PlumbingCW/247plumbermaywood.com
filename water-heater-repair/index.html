A leaking tank can flood a garage or closet quickly.

If you smell gas, leave the home and call the gas utility or 911 before calling us. No hot water with no leak is less urgent, but we still answer at night because cold mornings and sick kids do not wait.

Why Water Heaters Fail

Most tank heaters last about a decade, and the failures follow a pattern. A gas unit loses hot water when the pilot, thermocouple or gas valve stops working. An electric unit loses it when a heating element or thermostat burns out. Both types leak when the inside of the tank corrodes through.

Sediment makes everything worse. Minerals settle to the bottom of the tank, insulate the burner from the water and cause popping or rumbling noises. Hard water speeds this up, and published hardness in nearby supplies such as Commerce and South Gate is well above soft water, so a flush every year or so helps a tank last.

The anode rod inside the tank is a sacrificial part that corrodes before the steel does. Replacing it can add years to a heater that is not yet leaking, but only if it is checked before the tank wall is damaged.

Repair Or Replace: How To Decide

A plumber will weigh these points with you after looking at the unit.

  • Age of the heater, since units past about 10 years cost more to keep alive.
  • Whether the leak is at a fitting or from the tank body.
  • The cost of the repair compared with the cost of a new unit.
  • Whether parts are available for your model and brand.
  • Whether the heater is the right size for the household today.

Gas, Electric And Hybrid Units

Gas heaters need a clear flue and combustion air, and a plumber checks both on every repair because a blocked flue is a safety problem. Electric heaters need a steady power supply and correct breaker size, and a tripped breaker that keeps tripping means a failed element or a short.

Hybrid heat pump heaters add a compressor and a filter, and they behave differently from older tanks. If you own one, mention the brand when you call so the right plumber is sent. For a new unit, read the tankless water heater page as well.

Code And Rules To Know

California requires water heaters to be braced against earthquake movement, and the California Plumbing Code (based on the Uniform Plumbing Code, or UPC) sets rules for the relief valve and its discharge pipe. A repair visit is a good time to check both. Never cap or plug the relief valve.
