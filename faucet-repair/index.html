A Valve That Will Not Shut Off: Act Now

For emergency faucet repair in Maywood, call the 24/7 line. If a supply line is spraying or a faucet will not shut off, close the shutoff valve under the sink. If that valve is stuck or leaks, close the main water valve at the meter or where the line enters the house, then call.

A burst supply line under a sink can release many gallons in minutes and soak cabinets and flooring. Move anything you can away from the base, and keep towels handy.

Shower Valves, Tubs And Showerheads

A shower valve that drips or will not shut off is usually a worn cartridge, and swapping it is a short job if the valve is accessible. A leak inside the wall may need an access panel, which the plumber explains before cutting.

A tub faucet that runs hot only or cold only often has a failed cartridge or a mixing problem. A showerhead with weak flow is usually clogged by scale, and cleaning or replacing it is a quick fix. A shower or tub drain that clogs is covered under drain cleaning.

Burst Or Corroded Supply Valves

Shutoff valves under sinks and behind toilets are rarely used, and when they are, they can fail. Corroded valves leak at the stem or will not close, and a supply line can burst at its fitting. Replacing the valve and the line together prevents a repeat failure.

Hard water shortens the life of these parts. Published reports for nearby systems show hardness well above soft water, including Cal Water's average of 146 ppm for City of Commerce groundwater and 238.2 ppm for South Gate. We have not found a figure for the Maywood Mutual Water companies, so check your supplier's annual report. If the leak is bigger than a fixture, see burst pipe repair.

Code And Rules To Know

California appliance rules set flow limits for new faucets, so a replacement may flow less than the old one. The California Plumbing Code (based on the Uniform Plumbing Code, or UPC) also governs supply connections and drain traps under the sink. Ask the plumber to show the flow rating on the new faucet.
