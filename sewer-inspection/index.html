What A Sewer Camera Inspection Shows

A sewer inspection in Maywood sends a waterproof camera head down the line from a cleanout or a pulled toilet. It shows a live image on a screen and records video, so you and the plumber see the same thing. Roots, cracks, offsets, sags that hold water, scale, grease and collapses are all visible.

This matters because the cause of a clog is rarely visible from the surface. A drain that backs up twice a year may be a sag in the pipe or a root-filled joint, and cleaning the line again does nothing for that. The camera turns a guess into a diagnosis.

An inspection is a planned service, which is why this page uses standard wording. If your line is backing up now, start with the sewer line repair page for the 24/7 steps.

When An Inspection Makes Sense

The most common reasons we schedule one are these.

  • A drain has clogged more than once in a short period.
  • You are buying an older home and want to know the condition of the line.
  • Roots, tree work or construction happened close to the line.
  • You are planning a remodel that adds fixtures.
  • A previous repair needs to be verified.

Buying An Older Home

A general home inspection rarely includes the sewer line, since the pipe is underground. A camera inspection fills that gap. If a lateral has roots or a break, you can negotiate before closing or plan a repair.

Ask the seller's permission to access the cleanout. If there is no cleanout, a plumber can pull a toilet to feed the camera. The plumber tells you in advance what that will involve.

Neighbors in a block built in the same decade often share the same pipe material, so a camera finding at one house tells you what to watch for on the next. This is a pattern, not a rule, and each line is inspected on its own.

After The Inspection

If the line is clean and sound, you walk away with a clear answer and peace about it. If it is damaged, the plumber lays out the choices and prices each one, and nothing is done until you decide. Cleaning needs are covered under sewer line cleaning.

Code And Rules To Know

A camera inspection is not code work, but its findings are the basis for code work. Ask for the recording or still images, the footage length and the depth of any defect, and share them with the building department if a repair permit is needed. Repairs follow the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).
