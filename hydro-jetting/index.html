What Hydro Jetting Does That A Cable Cannot

Hydro jetting in Maywood uses a hose with a nozzle that fires high-pressure water in several directions inside the pipe. The water cuts through grease, scrapes scale off the walls and flushes the debris downstream, leaving the pipe close to its original diameter.

A cable machine works differently. It drills a hole through the blockage, and the pipe walls keep the grease and sludge that line them. That is why a drain can seem fixed for a few weeks and then clog again. Jetting removes the buildup, and the repeat clog usually goes away.

It is a stronger tool, so it is not always the right one. Pipe that is cracked, collapsed or badly corroded can be damaged by pressure, which is why a camera look often comes first. See sewer inspection for what that involves.

Backups That Call For Jetting Right Away

Call the 24/7 line if a main drain has backed up more than once, if sewage is coming up through a floor drain or tub, or if a restaurant or shop drain is full of grease. In those cases a plumber may recommend jetting after clearing the immediate blockage with a cable.

While you wait, stop all water use, keep pets and children away from overflow and do not pour chemicals down the drain. A jetter needs a clear access point, so tell the dispatcher where your cleanout is.

Where Jetting Pays Off

These are the situations where we recommend jetting most often.

  • Kitchen and restaurant lines that collect grease.
  • Older cast iron pipe with rough walls and scale.
  • Main lines with repeat soft root intrusion.
  • Apartment or duplex shared lines that clog every few months.
  • Lines where a snake got stuck or only opened a small hole.

Jetting On Commercial And Shared Lines

Shops and small restaurants along Slauson Avenue and Atlantic Boulevard share lines with neighbors, and one grease-heavy tenant can clog a whole building. A jetting visit on a set schedule often costs less than repeated emergency calls.

Commercial calls have a few extra details. Tell us the building type, how many units share the line and where the cleanout or grease interceptor sits. See commercial plumbing for the scope of those visits.

Code And Rules To Know

Jetting works inside lines governed by the California Plumbing Code (based on the Uniform Plumbing Code, or UPC) and by your city's sewer rules. A plumber should locate a cleanout before jetting and check pipe condition first, since high pressure can damage a pipe that is already cracked.
