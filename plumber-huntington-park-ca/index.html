Pacific Boulevard, Slauson And Randolph: Why Calls Here Are Different

Huntington Park was incorporated in 1906 and was planned as a commercial center and a stopping point for goods moving between Los Angeles and San Diego. The Pacific Electric line ran along what is now Randolph Street. That commercial history is still visible today in a dense city where shops, restaurants and homes sit side by side.

For plumbing, that density means two kinds of calls. Shops and restaurants along Pacific Boulevard, Santa Fe Avenue and Slauson Avenue call about grease, floor drains and water heaters. Homes on the side streets call about slow drains, old pipe and leaks. We send the plumber who suits each, and you can read how we handle shops on the commercial plumbing page.

Close quarters also mean shared walls and shared lines. A clog that shows up in your unit may start in a line you share with a neighbor, and the quickest way to a fix is to tell us the building type.

Water: What The City Report Shows

The City of Huntington Park publishes a Consumer Confidence Report, and its 2025 edition says the city pumps groundwater from local deep wells and also uses Metropolitan Water District of Southern California surface water. It lists total hardness at an average of 214 ppm for groundwater and 235 ppm for MWD water, which is roughly 12.5 and 13.7 grains per gallon by the standard conversion.

What that means for you: hard water leaves scale in water heaters, on faucets and inside tankless units. A flush and a look at the anode rod help a tank last, and the tankless water heater page explains scale care. Some addresses in the area buy from a different supplier, so check your bill for the name and ask for that supplier's report.

Before We Arrive: A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak

In Huntington Park, these four steps come first.

Stop the water

Find the shutoff, often at the meter box on the sidewalk side, and turn it clockwise.

Protect the electrics

Keep clear of outlets near the wet area and cut breakers only when you can reach them dry.

Save what you can

Lift stock, boxes and electronics off the floor. Shops should move inventory away from low shelves.

Ring the emergency line

Common Problems In Dense Blocks

These calls come up often in a city with this much shared infrastructure.

  • Backed-up kitchen drains in restaurants, caused by grease and food solids.
  • Water heater failures in apartment buildings serving several units.
  • Slow main drains where several units share a line.
  • Leaks behind walls shared between shops or units.
  • Toilets that run or leak at the base in older buildings.

Permits And Code In Huntington Park

Plumbing repairs in Huntington Park follow the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Water heater replacements, repipes and sewer work usually need a permit from the city building department, and the inspector checks the finished work. Ask your plumber who pulls the permit and keep the sign-off with your records.
