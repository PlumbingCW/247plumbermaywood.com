Start With A Test, Not A Sales Pitch

A water softener or filter solves specific problems. Hardness leaves scale on fixtures and shortens water heater life. Chlorine taste and sediment are different problems and need a different fix. Spending money before you know which one you have is the most common mistake.

Your water supplier publishes an annual water quality report, also called a Consumer Confidence Report. It lists hardness, minerals and test results for your system. Ask your supplier for the latest one, or look for it on the supplier website. A home test kit or a lab test checks your own tap.

We have not found a published hardness figure for the Maywood Mutual Water companies, so do not rely on a number from a neighboring city. Use your own supplier's report and a test of your tap.

Softener Or Filter: Which Does What

Carbon filterChlorine taste and odorHardness
Sediment filterParticles and rustDissolved minerals
Reverse osmosisMany dissolved solids at one tapWhole house flow without a large system

Check local rules before installing a softener that discharges brine, since some areas restrict it. Ask your city or water supplier what applies.

What To Check Before Buying

These steps keep you from paying for equipment you do not need.

  • Read your supplier's latest water quality report.
  • Test hardness at your own tap.
  • Note whether scale, taste or staining is the real problem.
  • Ask the plumber what the equipment will not fix.
  • Confirm space, drain access and electrical supply for the unit.

Service And Repair

If you already own a softener that is not working, we can diagnose it. Common problems include empty salt, a stuck valve, a clogged injector and a failed control. A plumber looks at settings, resin condition and drain flow before suggesting replacement.

A softener changes how a water heater lasts, so see water heater replacement for the full picture. For a tankless unit, scale is the main enemy and tankless water heater care is covered separately.

Code And Rules To Know

Some Southern California communities restrict salt-based softeners that send brine to the sewer, and rules differ by city and sanitation district. Ask the local sewer authority before you buy, and ask the seller for performance data, not only marketing claims. Installation follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).
