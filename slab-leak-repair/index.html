Water Under The Floor: Act Fast

For emergency slab leak repair in Maywood, call the 24/7 line. Close the main water valve and keep it closed until a plumber arrives. Do not run taps to see whether the leak is still there, since every gallon adds to the damage under the floor.

Look for a warm spot, damp carpet or baseboards, a sound of running water or a meter that spins with all taps off. Take photos and note when you noticed each sign.

What A Slab Leak Looks Like

A slab leak is a leak in a pipe under or inside a concrete floor. These signs usually point to one.

  • Warm or hot spots on the floor from a leaking hot water line.
  • Wet or discolored flooring, baseboards or carpet.
  • The sound of running water when all fixtures are off.
  • A water meter that moves with everything closed.
  • A sudden rise in the water bill.
  • Cracks in walls or floors that appear after a leak has undermined the soil.

Repair Options

Spot repair through the slab

The plumber cuts a small opening in the floor, exposes the pipe and repairs it. It works when the pipe is otherwise sound and the leak is one spot.

Reroute the line

The plumber abandons the leaking line under the slab and runs a new line through walls or the attic. It avoids breaking concrete and is useful when pipes under the slab are failing in several places.

Repipe

If the pipe is corroded throughout, replacing the system may make sense. See repiping for what that involves.

Why Slab Leaks Happen

Pipes under slabs can leak because of corrosion, movement in the soil, a poor fitting or friction against concrete. Pipe material and water chemistry also matter. Homes built in different eras use different pipe, and the age of the house helps show what is under the floor.

Not every house in this area has a slab foundation, and a plumber confirms what yours has. Raised-foundation homes have crawlspaces and different leak patterns, covered on the leak detection page. A slab leak applies only when the pipe is in or below concrete.

Hot water lines under a slab can leave a warm spot you can feel with a bare foot. Cold lines leave damp spots instead.

Safety Steps If You Suspect A Slab Leak

A slab leak can hide for weeks, so a few checks protect your family and floor.

  • Close the main valve if the floor is warm, wet or you hear running water with all taps off.
  • Keep electronics and cords away from damp floors and avoid using outlets near wet spots.
  • Roll back loose rugs and look for damp carpet edges, cracks or a musty smell.
  • Photograph any damage and your meter reading before and after you close the main.
  • Call a plumber with a leak locator rather than breaking concrete on a guess.

Code And Rules To Know

Slab leak repairs follow the California Plumbing Code (based on the Uniform Plumbing Code, or UPC) for pipe materials, and rerouting or repiping usually needs a permit. Ask your insurer about slab leak coverage before work starts, since policies differ on access and finish repairs.
