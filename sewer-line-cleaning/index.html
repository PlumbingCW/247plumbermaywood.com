Main Line Backup Right Now: First Steps

If you need emergency sewer line cleaning in Maywood, call the 24/7 line now. Stop using water, because every drain in the house feeds the same clogged line. The lowest tub, shower or floor drain usually overflows first, so keep that area clear.

Find your main cleanout, a capped pipe near the foundation or in the yard, and tell the dispatcher. A plumber who knows where it is can start faster, and you avoid pulling a toilet to reach the line.

How A Main Line Gets Clogged

Main line clogs come from the same few sources. Tree roots enter at joints. Grease and wipes build up inside bends. Heavy paper and flushable products clump at low spots. Occasionally a child's toy or a rag causes an instant blockage.

Roots are the usual cause on older lines. A tree does not need to be on your property, because roots follow water and sewage vapor through the ground. If a clog returns after a few months, ask about a camera look.

Shared lines in duplexes and small buildings add another layer. A clog downstream of several units may show up first in the lowest unit, which is not always where it started.

What To Do While You Wait

These actions limit the spread of wastewater.

  • Stop all water use, including washing machines and dishwashers.
  • Do not flush, even if the toilet seems to be draining.
  • Keep children and pets out of any wet area.
  • Clear a path to the cleanout so the plumber can get to it.
  • Move valuables off the floor of the lowest rooms.

Keeping A Main Line Clear

Habits matter. Put grease in a can instead of the drain, use a strainer in tubs and keep wipes and paper towels out of toilets. Those few changes prevent many of the backups we see.

If your line has been cleaned more than once in a year, schedule a camera inspection instead of paying for the same cleaning again. A line that keeps clogging usually has a physical problem, and the sooner it is found, the smaller the repair.

Code And Rules To Know

Cleaning a sewer line uses cleanouts that the California Plumbing Code (based on the Uniform Plumbing Code, or UPC) requires at set points. If your house has no accessible cleanout, adding one is a code-compliant improvement worth asking about, since it makes future cleaning faster.
